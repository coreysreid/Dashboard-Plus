# Australia: country-pack research notes

Checked **2026-11-09**. These are source notes, not emergency instructions or program content. A household must check its local authority and current conditions. Review by **2027-02-09**, or sooner if a source changes. We paraphrase and link; we do not copy guidance text.

## National body

The [Department of Home Affairs emergency-management page](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/about-us/our-portfolios/emergency-management), checked 2026-11-09, says it leads the Australian Government's disaster and emergency management response and names the **National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA)**. [Disaster Assist](https://www.disasterassist.gov.au/), also checked 2026-11-09, links to NEMA and to disaster information by state or territory. The NEMA destination was not reachable under the current network policy, so its own current guidance has not been verified.

## Phone numbers, hazards and household guidance

- **Emergency phone:** The [Queensland Government emergency services page](https://www.qld.gov.au/emergency/emergencies-services), checked 2026-11-09, says to call **Triple Zero (000)** for police, fire and ambulance in an emergency. The [SA Government emergency services page](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/emergencies-and-safety/emergency-services), checked the same date, also says to dial 000 for life-threatening or urgent situations. The Queensland page lists **132 500** for SES; this is a service number, not a replacement for 000.
- **Hazards:** The [Queensland Government disaster page](https://www.qld.gov.au/emergency/dealing-disasters), checked 2026-11-09, lists floods, bushfires, cyclones, storms, heatwaves and earthquakes among hazards affecting Queensland. This is a Queensland list, not a claim about every address in Australia.
- **Preparation:** The [Queensland Government preparation page](https://www.qld.gov.au/emergency/dealing-disasters/prepare-for-disasters), checked 2026-11-09, links to making a household emergency plan, preparing for evacuation and assembling an emergency kit. Its [kit page](https://www.qld.gov.au/emergency/dealing-disasters/prepare-for-disasters/emergency-kit), checked the same date, includes bottled water and says household members should know where the kit is kept. The checked kit page gives **no litres-per-person-per-day amount**, so this note does not supply one.
