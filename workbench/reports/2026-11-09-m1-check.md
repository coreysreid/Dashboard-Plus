# M1 household journey check — 2026-11-09

I ran the Linux development app with a fresh temporary data folder and made-up records, then rechecked the new Stock in/out flow after the 9 November update to `main`. This is a product walk-through through local HTTP requests, not a clean Windows portable-build test or a human usability session. Journeys are in `docs/JOURNEYS.md`; M1 promises are in `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

## Findings, worst first

### Important — the first-screen guidance is not the J1 wording

- **Did:** Finished the wizard with three made-up people and both shipped modules selected, then read the first home page.
- **Expected:** J1 says the home screen tells the household, “Add a few things from the pantry.”
- **Actual:** The wizard completed and the home page loaded, but that direction was absent.
- **Suggested change:** When Stock is on and has no items, show “Add a few things from the pantry” with a direct link to the Stock item form.

### Minor — J5's expiry examples can be hidden from the home summary

- **Did:** Added twenty low-stock items, then a synthetic water item due next week and a first-aid item due later this month.
- **Expected:** J5 describes the home screen naming the expiring items.
- **Actual:** Both appeared on the full attention page, but the home page showed only the first four items from the combined running-low and expiring list. The first-aid item was not named there. The home did show the count of expiring items and a link to the full page.
- **Suggested change:** Give expiring items their own short home-page line or show at least one of each attention type when both are present.

## What worked

- **J1:** The welcome page loaded; the wizard saved three made-up people and enabled People and Stock without file editing. The People grid showed all three afterward. The home page loaded. The complete ten-minute newcomer experience was not measured.
- **J2, partial:** “Save and add another” let a scripted local HTTP client submit twenty made-up stock items in **0.1 seconds**. That measures server response time only. On the updated `main`, I opened “Stock in and out,” recorded a synthetic “Bought or added” movement of four items, saw the count rise from two to six, and saw that item leave the running-low list. The earlier missing-movement finding is resolved. I did not verify type-ahead item selection or Enter-key behavior in a real browser, and a person must still type the details, so the under-ten-minute target remains unverified.
- **J3:** With three people and twenty litres of synthetic water, the home and attention pages showed about two days of water. The emergency sheet response included a synthetic person's name, and the supplies checklist rendered. Offline use and physical one-page printing were not checked.
- **J4:** Safe close created a close backup alongside a daily backup. After restarting the app, the health request returned HTTP 200 and the database still had three people and twenty-two stock items. A clean Windows USB move, separate parent wizard, second-copy backup and physical unplug were not tested.
- **J5:** The full attention page listed the synthetic expiring water and first-aid items. The home page listed the water item but not the first-aid item because the list was crowded with low-stock items.

The app bound to `127.0.0.1` during this run and closed cleanly. I did not assess the portable Windows build, printer pagination, a full day of operation, or every failure screen. All test names and stock records used here were invented; the temporary database was kept outside the repository.
