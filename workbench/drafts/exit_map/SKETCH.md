# A simple exit sketch from rooms and connections

Designed **21 November 2026** for batch 20 item 93. Decision 0103 already gives the household rooms/areas as shared `stock.places` and **Connections between places**, including a **Door to outside** kind. The first map uses that graph; it never asks for a measured floor plan, exact room size, GPS, photos or a building blueprint. “Lego-level” means labelled boxes and lines, one floor at a time.

## Least a household must enter

1. **Rooms and floors:** choose the main rooms people sleep in or pass through, with a floor for each. Use existing Places; nested cupboards and shelves stay off the map. Mark a room's type only if needed to distinguish sleeping rooms and circulation; do not infer from a name like “Study”. Empty rooms remain **Not mapped**.
2. **Ways between them:** confirm the existing decision 0103 connections, especially doors, stairs and openings. A line is a recorded connection, **not** proof that the door is open, accessible or fire-safe. An “Other” connection needs a household label before it is drawn as a usable way.
3. **Ways outside:** confirm which **Door to outside** lines reach a genuinely outside place. A door to a balcony, enclosed yard or shared corridor might not be an escape to safety; mark its destination as **Outside**, **Shared building area**, or **Needs checking**. For a possible window way out, the person adds a separate **Possible window exit · check locally** marker only after checking that it is suitable. Do not turn every room window into a route or give climbing instructions.
4. **Meeting place:** choose a broad, recognisable outside meeting place in the household's own words, with a privacy reminder. It is shown beside the map, not guessed from an address.
5. **People and building plan:** ask **Does anyone need help to leave?** with a link to a private household discussion, not names on the general map. For a flat or shared building, ask **What does your building tell residents to do in a fire?** Options **I have checked its plan**, **I need to check**, and **Not a shared building**; attach a broad reference such as “building notice” without copying an access code or inventing stay-put advice. The map cannot choose a flat's evacuation rule.

The page may draw before all five are complete, but it must title the result **Draft home sketch** and mark missing facts. **Ready to print for practice** requires the household to review every sleeping room, outside destination, meeting place, help needs and any shared-building instructions. This is a completeness check on *entered information*, not a safety certificate.

## On-screen words

Heading **Our way-out sketch**. Intro **This shows rooms and ways out that your household recorded. Check each route in person, practise together and follow your building's and local fire service's guidance. It is not an emergency instruction.** Button **Review rooms and ways out** opens Set up my home; **Add meeting place** asks for a broad description. Each floor is a labelled box group; rooms are rectangles, connections are labelled lines (**Door**, **Stairs**, **Open doorway**, **Door to outside**). A marked exit gets an arrow **Possible way outside · check in person**, not **Safe exit**. Unconnected rooms say **No way out recorded for this room**; one-way rooms say **Only one way out recorded. Check for another possible way with your household.** If a room has two graph paths that share the same hallway or door, do **not** count them as two independent ways out. The map cannot determine that from the current data.

Beside the drawing: **Outside meeting place: Letterbox at the front path** (invented Smith example), **Help to leave: Discussed privately / Needs discussion**, **Building plan: Check the notice from your building manager** when relevant. A flat banner says **This sketch does not replace your building's fire plan. Some buildings use different instructions for fires inside or outside your flat; check with the responsible manager or local fire service.** There is no generated “shortest route” or timed evacuation estimate.

Text alternative below the drawing: **From Main bedroom: Door to Hallway. From Hallway: Stairs to Front hall. From Front hall: Door to outside (destination needs checking).** Each line is a link back to its source record. A screen reader gets floor headings and this ordered connection list, not only visual arrows. At 320 px the drawing can scroll *within its own labelled region*, while the text alternative and actions stay usable without horizontal page scrolling.

**Print:** one floor per page where possible, plus a text connection list, date last reviewed, meeting place and incomplete markers. Never print Home layout access details, spare-key locations, private People details, codes or utility account references. Before print show **This page shows your home's layout. Keep copies where your household wants them.** A sheet from unreviewed connections says **Draft · check routes in person** on every page. The ordinary export and helper access already expose room and connection names under decision 0103, so the map should not pretend the layout is secret.

## Worked invented example and limits

For Smith's invented home: boxes **Main bedroom → Hallway → Stairs → Front hall → Door to outside**. The computer may draw that chain from Places and Connections, but it cannot claim a second bedroom route or that the front door is usable. A second drawn line to the same front hall is not independent if both depend on the same stairs. A person must add/check another possible way and practise it. For Taylor's invented flat, show the apartment rooms and connections only; the building's own evacuation instructions remain **Needs checking** until the household records that they looked at them. No real home layout is used.
