# Compare two things without pretending to know why

Batch 24 item 132, planned **27 November 2026**. This is a proposed view, not a shipped chart. It compares records for the **same subject** over the same dates while keeping each measure's unit, missing entries, privacy choice and evidence in view. It never turns an association into a cause, a diagnosis or feeding advice.

## What the household chooses and sees

1. **Compare two things** starts with one subject, one period and two compatible measures. The first safe pair is **Rex: food put out and weight**. The picker explains **Food put out is what you recorded placing in a bowl or giving as a treat. It does not say what Rex ate.** A different pet, unit or missing sharing permission cannot be joined by a similar name.
2. The view opens with this permanent caption: **These records happened in the same period. Food put out is not food eaten. This chart cannot tell why Rex's weight changed.** It names the selected dates and how many food-recording days and weigh-ins exist. It never says a diet, medicine or visit *caused* a change. A household note or vet visit may appear as a separately labelled recorded event, not as an explanation chosen by the program.
3. Two vertically aligned lines share one **date axis**. The upper line plots **recorded grams put out per confirmed complete week**; its scale starts at zero and breaks at an incomplete week. The lower line plots Rex's **recorded kg weigh-ins on their actual dates**, with a visible scale starting at zero. It breaks across a weight gap longer than six weeks. Each panel has its own unit and no second axis is superimposed on the other. Hover, focus and print repeat the date, unit, included rows and coverage words. Colour is never the sole distinction.
4. **See the records in a table** is equally usable without the graphic: one row per week, headed **Week starting**, **Food entries**, **Food put out (g)**, **Food log complete?**, **Weigh-in date and weight (kg)**, **Used in comparison?**. A blank weight cell reads **No weigh-in recorded**, not zero. An incomplete food week reads **Partial — not compared**, with its recorded subtotal still visible. The table links to permitted source records and prints with the caption and units.

## A minimum before drawing the comparison

The first comparison line needs **at least 12 household-confirmed complete food-recording weeks** within the period from the first to last weigh-in, **at least six distinct weigh-in dates spanning 12 weeks**, and a weigh-in in both the first and last third of that overlap. A food week with unknown coverage is partial even if it has seven dated rows; a week with no rows is unknown, not zero. One weigh-in on a day is a point; repeated readings on the same day remain separate records and do not manufacture extra dates. All relevant records must be accepted, not waiting on Review. Until the gate is met, show the separate existing food and weight charts with their records, then: **Not enough complete weeks and dated weights to compare yet. Food: 3 of 12 complete weeks; weight: 2 of 6 dated weigh-ins. Nothing is being counted as zero.** Substitute the real counts and explain a sharing choice that hides either measure.

This is a display gate, **not** a claim that 12 weeks prove a relationship. The first version computes no correlation coefficient, trend verdict, prediction or health alert. A scatter plot would need a clear rule for pairing a weekly food amount to a weight measured on a different day; do not interpolate or use the nearest reading by stealth. Keep it out of the first version. The period can be changed, but the gate and coverage recalculate and an incomplete recent week is never compared to a full earlier one.

## Rex's invented year, worked through

The three CSVs here are **new fictional planning data**, not the shipped Smith household. Shipped Rex has three weigh-ins and two feeding entries, insufficient for this view; batch 23's multi-year demo pet is Biscuit, not Rex. The invented records run from **Monday 6 January 2025 through Sunday 4 January 2026**. [rex_feedings.csv](rex_feedings.csv) has one 07:30 food-put-out entry on each of 363 days; [rex_weigh_ins.csv](rex_weigh_ins.csv) has 12 actual dated weights, all on Monday week starts. The weekly view's weight cell therefore shows a weight only where a real weigh-in falls on that week start; it is not a calculated weekly weight. Example amounts are made up, not advice about a dog's food. [rex_weekly_view.csv](rex_weekly_view.csv) is a derived review table with **51 made-up household confirmations** of complete weeks. The week starting 1 September is deliberately unconfirmed and has no entry for 3 September. The shipped app has no week-completeness field, so the confirmed column is an explicit proposed input, not a fact inferred from seven rows.

| Date or span | Exact invented records | What the view may say |
|---|---|---|
| 6 Jan to 6 Jul: 26 complete weeks | One 300 g entry each day, **7 × 300 = 2,100 g recorded per week**; Rex was 21.3 kg on 6 Jan and 22.0 kg on 23 Jun. | “Food put out: 2,100 g in each of 26 complete weeks before 7 July.” Keep both actual weigh-in dates visible. |
| 7 Jul onward, except one partial week | One 250 g entry each day, **7 × 250 = 1,750 g** in each of 25 complete weeks. | “Food put out: 1,750 g in each of 25 complete weeks from 7 July.” Do not treat the unmatched 26th week as zero. |
| Week starting 1 Sep | Six 250 g entries = **1,500 g recorded**; 3 Sep has no entry and the household did not confirm coverage. | “Partial week: 1,500 g recorded on six days. Not compared with complete weeks.” |
| 21 Jul to 15 Sep | 21.9 kg on 21 Jul; no 18 Aug weigh-in; 21.5 kg on 15 Sep. | Keep two dots and a gap, never a guessed August weight or a continuous line across eight weeks. |
| 8 Dec | Rex weighed 20.8 kg; the last three feeding weeks extend beyond that last weigh-in. | “Rex's recorded weight was 1.2 kg lower than on 23 June. Food entries continue after the last weight; there is no later weight to compare them with.” |

The exact year caption is: **“For Rex, recorded food put out was 2,100 g in each of 26 complete weeks before 7 July, and 1,750 g in each of 25 complete weeks after. One September week is partial. Rex weighed 22.0 kg on 23 June and 20.8 kg on 8 December. The records moved in the same period; they do not tell us what Rex ate or why his weight changed.”** The first-to-last weight overlap contains more than 12 confirmed weeks and six dated weights, so the display gate passes. It still cannot establish cause. Do not include a suggestion to change feeding or medication.

## Other pairs, later

| Pair | Honest boundary before it can appear |
|---|---|
| Doses recorded and a health reading | Medicine doses are private and behind Show; a medication plan is not a record that a dose was given. A matching person's reading type, unit and dates must exist. The household's sharing choices govern each list. Never turn an absent dose entry into “missed” or a health conclusion. A reading module and consent-safe comparison are future work. |
| Spending and a monthly measure | Stock's Spent per month includes only person-confirmed priced Stock ins, less recorded money back; it is **not** all household spending. Finance transactions, if built, need one currency, explicit coverage, refund/transfer rules and duplicate protection. Never mix a partial Stock total with a full Finance total or call a gap zero. |
| Two arbitrary lists | Offer only declared, reviewed measure pairs with the same subject and compatible period. No free-form SQL, private field in a label, unit conversion by guess, or automatically selected “interesting” relationship. |

## Checks before promotion

1. Recalculate `rex_weekly_view.csv` from the two source CSVs: 363 feeding rows, 12 weigh-in rows, 51 complete weeks, and one 1,500 g partial week. A correction, deletion or pending Review row changes only the appropriate week; the table and graphic agree.
2. Remove the 3 September gap, but leave the household confirmation unset: it stays partial. Confirming the week deliberately makes it eligible; retracting confirmation removes it again. Zero is never inferred from no entries.
3. Move the 18 August weigh-in back in, then remove it; the weight line joins only the actual nearby dates and breaks across the eight-week gap. No weekly weight is invented.
4. Test two pets both called Rex with distinct IDs, mixed food units (g, pouch, scoop), a weigh-in in kg and a new entry in another local time zone. No name guess, unit sum or wrong household day reaches the view.
5. With five weigh-in dates, eleven confirmed weeks, or no weight in the final third, show only the separate records and the exact missing counts. A newly staged feeding on Review never satisfies the gate.
6. Put private dose or money data in the second picker and switch its list to **Only on this computer**. Neither the choice, chart, table, URL, print, export, helper view nor member snapshot reveals its value or existence contrary to decision 0140. A Show action is deliberate and scoped to that view.
7. At 320 px, desktop, large text, keyboard and screen reader, the caption and table give the same evidence as the lines; print in light/dark/high contrast with units and missing-week labels. Neither the two scales nor colour alone suggests causation.
