# Country pack content draft

These four files are **linked, dated content**, not a live country-pack module. Every emergency number, body, water amount and hazard has its own official source and check date. A hazard list means that the cited body discusses the topic; it does not say the hazard applies to a household's address. Each pack ends with a local-authority question, as decision 0025 requires. Review dates are editorial maintenance targets, not public advice.

## Scope of the water values

- **AU:** NSW Rural Fire Service's emergency kit recommends at least **3 litres per person per day**. This is a NSW source; the pack labels it as such. The existing Queensland research gave no per-day figure. Local and household needs may differ.
- **US:** CDC says at least **1 US gallon per person per day** for a three-day emergency supply. The unit is kept as published, so the program must convert before comparing litre totals.
- **UK:** The UK Be Ready campaign recommends **3 litres of drinking water per person per day for three days** on its water-supply interruption page.
- **NZ:** Get Ready says **3 litres per person per day** without water.

These are source-specific starting figures, not a universal safety threshold. The core should show the source, unit and date beside any calculated days of water. It must count only water the household confirms is drinking water and follow local advice where it differs.

## Reuse and checks

AU links and paraphrases Home Affairs, Queensland Government and NSW Rural Fire Service. Home Affairs material is generally CC BY 3.0 AU; Queensland Government is generally CC BY 4.0, with exceptions; NSW RFS terms for this page were not confirmed. The AU `reuse_source` points to Queensland's copyright page and is **not** a blanket licence for the other authorities. US federal government text is generally public domain under 17 U.S.C. 105, with third-party exceptions. UK GOV.UK content is generally Open Government Licence 3.0, with exceptions. NZ Get Ready Crown content is generally CC BY 4.0, with logos and design excluded; NZ Police and NEMA site terms were not confirmed. All content here is paraphrased and linked, without reproduced guidance or imagery. Checked **2026-11-16**. Existing source notes in `workbench/research/country-packs/` give further regional limitations.

## Core work

The country-pack loader needs a versioned schema, per-value citations, link checks and expiry/review warnings. A household must be able to see the authority behind a value and open the current guidance. Country is not enough to decide local hazards; use household input or an explicitly chosen local authority. Emergency numbers must remain visible offline, with the pack's last-checked date. Do not silently override the household's water basis when a pack is installed. The present four files do not include state or local phone lists; those remain research work, not values to guess.

## Round 2 jurisdiction index, checked 2026-11-17

[`ROUND2.csv`](ROUND2.csv) adds 38 jurisdiction rows: all eight Australian states and territories, the [10 most populous US states in the Census Bureau's 2025 estimates](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/population-growth-slows.html), all four UK nations, and the [16 New Zealand CDEM areas](https://www.civildefence.govt.nz/about/contact-us/find-your-emergency-management-committee). Every row names a public emergency body, warning channel, hazard topic and water guidance, with a URL for each. All 52 distinct URLs returned HTTP 200 on a 2026-11-17 check. This checks access, not the future accuracy of dynamic warning data. Review each row by **2027-02-17** or sooner if an authority changes its service.

The `hazard_scope` and `water_scope` columns are essential. For US rows, the [National Weather Service hazard menu](https://www.weather.gov/safety/) is a national topic list, not proof that flood risk applies at an address. UK rows use the [Be Ready UK hazard menu](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/be-informed-about-hazards/) in the same limited way; NZ rows use [Get Ready emergency topics](https://getready.govt.nz/emergency/). Australian rows link a state warning or emergency page for the named topic, but even a state topic does not establish local risk. A future pack must ask for the household's local authority and risk map rather than automatically activating a hazard from this CSV.

Water sources are the existing four country-pack sources, made explicit in every row. In AU, the **3 litres/person/day** citation is from [NSW Rural Fire Service](https://www.rfs.nsw.gov.au/plan-and-prepare/prepare-your-family/emergency-survival-kit), so NSW is the only row that calls it local. The seven other AU rows say “NSW guidance only; local figure unverified”; the core must not represent that amount as their state government's advice. US rows cite [CDC's one-US-gallon starting point](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-create-and-store-an-emergency-water-supply.html). UK rows cite [Be Ready's three litres](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/be-informed-about-hazards/water-supply-interruptions-or-outages/). NZ rows cite [Get Ready's three litres](https://getready.govt.nz/prepared/household/supplies/storing-water). All were rechecked 2026-11-17. These are attributed starting figures, not a universal safety threshold; they do not replace the household's own water setting or current local advice.

**Warning coverage:** Australia has state or territory portals or apps (ACT ESA, Hazards Near Me NSW, SecureNT, Queensland disaster warnings, Alert SA, TasALERT, VicEmergency and Emergency WA). US rows use a state alert page where directly accessible; Texas, Illinois and Ohio use the [National Weather Service warning-dissemination page](https://www.weather.gov/about/warning-dissemination) as a national channel and require a local service check. Ohio's agency site returned HTTP 404 from this environment, so its body is cited via [USAGov's state-agency directory](https://www.usa.gov/state-emergency-management); do not publish an Ohio-specific alert URL until it can be checked. Florida's AlertFlorida URL returned 403 here, so the table uses the accessible official Florida Disaster weather page instead. The UK uses the [UK Emergency Alerts service](https://www.gov.uk/alerts) for all four nations; local flood warnings differ by nation. NZ uses the national [Emergency Mobile Alert](https://getready.govt.nz/prepared/ema) and points people to their local CDEM group via the body directory. No app install is required by this draft.

**Reuse and licences:** `ROUND2.csv` contains links and short factual paraphrases only, no copied warning feed, maps, logos or instructions. Existing AU research in `workbench/research/country-packs/AU.md` records ACT/NSW/QLD/SA CC BY terms, NT's lack of a blanket CC licence, Tasmania's non-commercial site licence, WA's limited information-access terms, and unverified VicEmergency terms (checked 9–13 Nov 2026). Treat NT, Tasmania, WA and unknown Australian pages as **link only**; their terms do not permit broad reuse here. US federal material is generally public domain under [17 U.S.C. 105](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html), checked 2026-11-17, but state site licences have not all been verified, so those are link only. GOV.UK material is generally [Open Government Licence 3.0](https://www.gov.uk/help/terms-conditions), with exceptions; nation-specific sites need their own terms checked before copying. Get Ready Crown text is generally [CC BY 4.0](https://getready.govt.nz/about-this-site/), excluding logos and marked third-party content; local CDEM sites have separate terms. Nothing from a restricted or unknown-licence page is reproduced here beyond isolated facts and links.

The country-pack loader still needs a versioned subregion schema, per-field source labels and dates, review expiry, regional selection, and a prompt to check a household's specific address. The CSV is a research handoff until those features and local hazard and water checks exist. Do not show a national topic as a local alert or a cross-state water value as a recommendation from the household's state.
