# Home inventory promotion notes

Checked **2026-11-17**. The [US National Association of Insurance Commissioners' Home Inventory page](https://content.naic.org/consumer/home-inventory) says an inventory can group belongings by room or category, include pictures and be exported, and describes the inventory as information useful to an insurer. This draft stores **where** a household keeps its photos and receipts; it does not upload images or assert what a particular insurer will accept. The room-by-room, serial, cost and evidence fields are also Corey's batch 8 item 25 request. The example costs and serials are invented.

**Privacy:** Room paths, serials, evidence locations and private notes together form a sensitive map of valuables. `serial` and `notes` are marked sensitive. The list omits them; the explicitly chosen claim sheet includes serials and warns about handling the copy. The core must confirm that `sensitive` fields are protected consistently in sheets, exports, helper views, backups and any future APIs. The present sheet contract has no per-sheet access or print-confirmation flag, so Lane A should add that before offering the claim printout. Do not include this sheet in routine emergency print bundles. Storage locations for photos/receipts should be broad descriptions, never passwords, file-system paths to private accounts, or embedded files.

**Core needs:** `order_by = "room"` may sort by a link ID rather than the full nested path. The claim sheet needs grouping and sorting by the computed full place path, plus repeated room headings and safe page breaks. If Home layout becomes the owner of Places, migrate the link with an explicit ambiguous-name review and preserve the inventory across backup/restore. The `money` type uses the household currency; never aggregate across currencies or call paid cost a valuation. No attachment handling is in this draft.

**Reuse:** NAIC page wording is paraphrased and linked, not copied; checked 2026-11-17. Its guidance is US general information, not a claim rule for AU, UK or NZ. Each household must check its own insurer's terms when making a claim.
