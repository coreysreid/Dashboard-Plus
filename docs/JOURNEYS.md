# Journeys

Short stories of real use. Each is an acceptance test: when its milestone ships,
a person should be able to do exactly this, and it should feel the way it says.
Names and details are invented.

## J1. The first ten minutes (M1)
Sam unzips the folder from a USB stick and double-clicks *Open Dashboard*. The
browser opens on a welcome page, not an error. Sam adds the three people who
live there, says it is a two-storey house in their country, and picks People and
Household stock. The home screen says what to do first: "Add a few things from
the pantry." Sam adds rice, tinned tomatoes, bottled water and batteries with a
minimum for each.
**Feels like:** quick, calm, no jargon, no file editing. Ten minutes, done.

## J2. The shopping comes home (M1 typed; M2 from a receipt)
On Saturday the bags come in. Sam opens *Stock in*, types the first letters of
each item, the quantity, and presses Enter to add the next. Expiry dates are
optional and remembered from last time. The *Running low* list shrinks.
**Feels like:** faster than writing a list on the fridge.
In M2, Sam can type the receipt as one purchase, or a helper can propose the
lines for Sam to approve.

## J3. The power has been out since last night (M1)
Sam opens the dashboard on a laptop running on battery. The home screen shows
"about 2 days of water for 3 people" and where it is kept. The emergency sheet,
printed last month, is on the fridge with the neighbour's number and the
doctor's. Nothing needed the internet.
**Feels like:** reassurance. The answers were already written down.

## J4. Handing it on (M1, restore in M2)
Sam's parent wants the same thing. Sam copies the folder to a new USB stick
without the `data` folder, and the parent starts their own wizard. Later, Sam's
own stick fails; the backup on the computer still has last night's copy.
**Feels like:** it belongs to you, and it is hard to lose.

## J5. Something expires (M1)
On Monday the home screen shows "Expiring soon: 2 bottles of water (next week),
first aid cream (this month)". Sam moves the water to the kitchen to use it and
buys fresh.
**Feels like:** a helpful nudge, not an alarm.

## J6. "Have you thought about…?" (M3)
A prompt asks: "If the stairs were blocked, could everyone get down from upstairs?"
Sam taps *Not yet, remind me*, then walks the house that weekend and records a
plan. The coverage view moves from "1 of 9 looked at" to "2 of 9".
**Feels like:** a thoughtful friend, not a lecture.

## J7. The vet asks how long it has been going on (M5, needs M4)
The vet asks when the cat started eating less. Sam opens the pet's food chart,
which shows the dip starting six weeks ago next to a confirmed note: "new food
brand". Sam shows the vet.
**Feels like:** the records paid off.
