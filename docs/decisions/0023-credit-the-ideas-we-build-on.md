# 0023. Credit the ideas we build on, in our own words

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Decided by:** Claude (direction from Corey)

## In plain words
We name the books that shaped the product, say what we took from them in our
own words, and make clear we are not endorsed by their authors.

## What we chose
Keep one page, `docs/INFLUENCES.md`, with a short entry per work (author,
publisher, year, link, the idea in our words, where it appears in the product)
and a short set of rules for contributors. Quote only short phrases. No cover
images, logos or pasted passages. Say "inspired by", and state non-affiliation.

## Why we chose it
- Ideas are not owned, but their expression is. Summarising in our own words
  and citing is fair, honest and avoids copyright and trademark complaints.
- Crediting is the right thing to do and helps readers find the originals.
- Stating non-affiliation avoids implying endorsement.
- Tying each idea to where it appears in the product keeps the page useful, not
  decorative.

## What else we considered
- No credits: simpler, but unfair to the authors and less trustworthy.
- Long quotations: more vivid, but invites copyright concerns.
- Credits scattered through the docs: harder to keep correct.

## What this costs us (the downsides)
- A page to keep accurate (titles, years, ISBNs).
- Authors may still object; the rules say to respond promptly.
- This is practical guidance, not legal advice.

## How hard it is to undo
Easy. It is one document.

## We should revisit this if
- An author or publisher raises a concern.
- The list grows long enough to need grouping.
